Hypothetical Thought Experiment

What If IT Was
Not Part of Our Current Life?

Imagine Tomorrow Without IT:

“Imagine waking up tomorrow and discovering that software, digital payments, online services, cloud systems, modern communication systems and many technology-based services are no longer available.”

Notice: This is a hypothetical scenario created to understand how deeply IT is connected to modern life. It is not a prediction of the future, but a diagnostic tool to realize our dependency.
Systemic Impact

What Would Happen?

Examining the domino effect across the 5 bedrock sectors of daily life when digital infrastructure disappears.

Banking

CRITICAL DISRUPTION

Without IT, the financial architecture that handles billions of instant daily micro-settlements would freeze:

  • Digital payments would stop — UPI, card swipe machines, and internet payments cease completely.
  • Online banking would be unavailable — Mobile apps and net banking portals go offline.
  • Financial transactions would become slower — Physical cash, paper cheques, and massive branch queues return.
  • Businesses would face major disruption — Inability to settle payrolls, vendor invoices, or trade credits.

Healthcare

CRITICAL DISRUPTION

When life-support data networks drop, patient diagnostics and triage are critically undermined:

  • Digital medical systems could be affected — Electronic records, MRI/CT scanners, and digital lab reports become inaccessible.
  • Online consultations could stop — Remote doctors and telemedicine links for rural clinics vanish.
  • Hospital management systems could face disruption — ICU bed allocation, pharmacy inventories, and emergency dispatch spiral into confusion.

Education

CRITICAL DISRUPTION

The democratized learning revolution halts, reinstating severe geographic divides:

  • Online classes would disappear — No remote video lectures, live mentoring sessions, or remote bootcamps.
  • Digital learning platforms would be unavailable — Edtech portals, interactive coding sandboxes, and LMS servers disappear.
  • Students would lose access to many online resources — Global research repositories, open-source documentation, and e-libraries shut down.

Transportation

CRITICAL DISRUPTION

The arteries of the country grind to a crawl under manual paper dispatch:

  • Online ticket booking could stop — IRCTC rail reservation, flight bookings, and bus apps shut down, causing chaos at physical counters.
  • Navigation systems could be affected — GPS map routing, ride-hailing services, and traffic coordination fail.
  • Logistics and delivery systems would face difficulties — Fleet tracking, package sortation, and grocery delivery networks collapse.

Business & Commerce

CRITICAL DISRUPTION

The enterprise engine stalls as interconnected corporate software goes dark:

  • E-commerce would be heavily affected — Warehouses cannot fulfill orders, payments fail, and online storefronts go blank.
  • Software-based business operations would slow down — Enterprise ERP, inventory controls, and payroll calculations stall.
  • Communication and collaboration would become difficult — Loss of email, instant messaging, cloud file sharing, and remote conference tools.
Talent Ecosystem Impact

What Happens to Engineers and Developers?

If IT becomes weak or disappears, the impact would not only affect software companies. It would reverberate through millions of families, educational institutions, and national aspirations.

Fewer Software Jobs

Campus placements and IT campus hiring waves shrink drastically, leaving hundreds of thousands of annual computer science graduates without direct absorption.

Reduced Demand for Development Skills

Specialized knowledge in modern web stacks, mobile apps, database optimization, and cloud architecture faces a temporary or permanent collapse in market value.

Fewer Startup Opportunities

India’s thriving venture ecosystem and unicorn factory dries up as venture capital retreats from software services, leaving founders unable to raise capital.

Less Innovation

Domestic patent filings, AI research initiatives, and open-source contributions slow down, stalling India's transition into a global technology innovator.

Reduced Global Opportunities

Indian engineers lose the immense advantage of remote high-paying international contracts, global product development centers, and foreign exchange earnings.

Pressure to Move Into Other Careers

Engineers are forced into intense career switching, creating severe bottlenecks in competitive public examinations, retail banking, and unrelated industries.

The Public Sector Appeal

Why Might More Engineers Focus on Government Jobs?

When IT careers become uncertain or volatile, some engineering graduates naturally migrate toward government examinations. Why does this shift happen?

Job Stability

Insulated from corporate layoffs, market recessions, and sudden global software downturns.

Structured Career Paths

Clear hierarchical promotions governed by civil service rules and transparent seniority ladders.

Job Security

Permanent tenure until retirement age, providing peace of mind to candidates and their families.

Social Recognition

Deep cultural prestige and respect across Indian society, particularly in regional communities.

Predictable Working Conditions

Regulated working hours, clear job scopes, and absence of 24/7 on-call software incident duties.

Comprehensive Benefits

Government quarters, medical schemes, pension structures, and family allowances.

Crucial Perspective: “Government jobs are an important career choice, but if a large number of engineers completely move away from technology, India could face a severe shortage of skilled technology professionals needed to maintain its sovereignty and competitiveness.”

The Independent Route

What About Freelancing?

Some engineers and developers choose freelancing as an alternative to formal corporate employment. But what is the honest reality behind this path?

The Opportunities & Freedom

  • Flexible Working: Set your own working schedule and work from any town or city.
  • Remote Opportunities: No geographic barriers; eliminate grueling commutes.
  • Independent Work: Direct autonomy over the projects and technologies you work on.
  • International Clients: Ability to invoice in foreign currencies (USD, EUR, GBP) from India.
  • Multiple Income Streams: Diversify earnings across multiple clients rather than a single employer.

The Real Challenges

  • Finding High-Quality Clients: Ongoing client acquisition requires strong sales and pitching skills.
  • Irregular Income: Feast-or-famine cycles where one month is lucrative and the next is dry.
  • Intense Global Competition: Competing against millions of global developers on freelance marketplaces.
  • Need for Self-Marketing: You must act as your own marketer, accountant, and project manager.
  • Continuous Skill Upgrades: Freelancers who stop learning are replaced by faster, cheaper alternatives.

“Freelancing is an opportunity, but it still depends on strong technical skills and a strong IT ecosystem.”

Without modern digital payment gateways, international bank transfers, cloud deployment servers, and clients who need software built, freelancing cannot exist. Freelancing does not replace the IT ecosystem—it flourishes upon it.

The Domino Effect

“If IT Becomes Weak, the Problem Is Bigger Than Losing Software Jobs.”

The collapse of technology does not stop inside engineering colleges. It triggers a vicious economic chain reaction that impacts national GDP, self-reliance, and citizen welfare.
